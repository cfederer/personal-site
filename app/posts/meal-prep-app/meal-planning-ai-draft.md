# I Didn't Need AI to Plan My Meals. I Needed It to Help Me Decide.

I've been doing the same mealplanning routine for the last decade (at least) of my life. I'd open a spreadsheet full of recipes I'd collected over the years and stare at it for far longer than I'd like to admit. I had about 4 recipes I honestly knew I'd pick from because I made them every time. And then I had about 20 recipes I thought sounded good at some point so I put them on my list and literally never made them. 

I spent way too much time reading 3582 meals for dinner, etc. One thing I finally accepted was that while I want a little variety, I have a type and I wanted things more in my range. Too manyrecipes didn't really fit how oru family works. i.e. they'd use ingredients I'd
never buy again or require an hour of prep on a Tuesday night.

## My first attempt automated the wrong thing

I'm embarrassed to say that I started off making the exact same mistake that I see over and over again right now with AI. Shoot for the moon, automate everything end to end, find out it's not usual, throw project away. 

So what I did was made an app that generated my week of meals for me, added the items to instacart, and then I'd just have to review and click 'order' and my job was done. But as you might expect, I did not like it. I did not always feel like what AI picked out for me. It didn't take into account that I had leftover tacos from a dinner out and I didn't want to make them again the same night (even though AI is right, I make tacos almost every week). 

Then, I was finding the instacart connector to be a bit buggy. And it was adding salt to my cart every time because every recipe needs salt. And I happen to already have salt. So if it didn't one-shot work end to end, I should probably throw it away....

## What did I really want to automate? And what didn't I?

This made me take a step back and think about what I actually wanted to automate and get help with, and where I wanted control. And if it idn't work end to end, what steps were actually helpful for me to save time? 

What I really needed help with was making decisions and generating good ideas for me to select from. 

So every week the app now does two things:

-   It selects a portion of the options from recipes I already know I
    love.
-   It searches for new recipes that match my preferences: high
    protein, no red meat, simple weeknight meals, and generally the same
    style of cooking I already enjoy.

Now I have a curated set of familiar favorites mixed with recipes that
feel like something I'd probably discover myself.

That's a much better starting point than either staring at a spreadsheet
or scrolling endless recipe websites.

When I find one I love, I can save it back into my permanent recipe
library. Over time, the app gradually learns my taste because **I'm**
the one deciding what gets promoted into my collection.

## The grocery list was the easy part

Once I pick the meals for the week, the app builds a grocery list
automatically. I stopped there this time. In part because we are trying to get to the grocery more instead of using Instacart. But legit, we HATE the grocery. 

Once I fine-tuned the recipes, the number of choices I wanted, etc, I went back to adding in the options to actually add items to an my Instacart cart. But this really wasn't what I NEEDED to make this app useful. 

And I will likely never actually use this truly end to end without being in the loop. Recipes don't know what's already in my pantry. They don't know I bought
soy sauce yesterday or that I still have half a bag of spinach left.

## What building this taught me about AI

This little side project ended up teaching me something I think applies
to AI products far beyond meal planning.

The most valuable systems aren't the ones that replace every decision.

They're the ones that remove the boring work while leaving people in
control of the decisions that actually benefit from context, preference,
and judgment.

In my case, AI is great at:

-   Searching thousands of recipes.
-   Finding new ideas that fit my taste.
-   Organizing grocery lists.
-   Remembering recipes I've approved.

I'm still better at deciding what sounds good for dinner this Tuesday.
